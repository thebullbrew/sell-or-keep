How the math works

Selling means proceeds after the mortgage payoff, agent commissions, closing costs and capital-gains tax — then that cash compounds in the market at your assumed return.

Keeping means your equity compounds through appreciation and amortization, plus every year's after-tax cash flow reinvested at that same market return. Comparing wealth, not monthly cash flow, is the only honest frame — a rental can lose money monthly and still win on equity.

Depreciation is the quiet hero: the building (not the land) writes off over 27.5 years, creating a paper loss that shields rental income from taxes. The IRS takes some back as depreciation recapture when you eventually sell — this tool doesn't model recapture, so keeping's edge is slightly overstated on long holds.

The exclusion clock: live in it 2 of the last 5 years and up to $250k/$500k of gain is tax-free. Move out and the clock starts — roughly 3 years later the exclusion is gone. If your gain is big, that clock alone can be the deciding factor.

Simplified on purpose: no state-by-state tax rules, no PMI, no refinancing, no "tenant trashes the place" premium. Not tax advice — run the final version past your CPA.
